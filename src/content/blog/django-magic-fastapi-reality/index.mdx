This week I gave a talk at EuroPython 2026 called **Django Magic, FastAPI Reality: Test Isolation at Scale**. It's the live, slide-shaped version of the ground covered on this blog in the [test client](/blog/test-client-python-tests-1) [series](/blog/test-client-python-tests-2) [and](/blog/test-client-python-tests-3) the post on [SQLAlchemy sessions](/blog/sqlalchemy-sessions): how Django cleans up the database between tests without you noticing, why FastAPI doesn't get that for free, and what your actual options are once you decide to fix it yourself.

## What's in it

- Why FastAPI's test client is "just" an HTTPX client while Django's hooks straight into the view, and why that difference matters for isolation
- How `transaction.atomic()` chains transactions and savepoints to make Django's test rollback invisible
- What it actually takes to get the same rollback behavior out of FastAPI and SQLAlchemy, and where it falls short of Django's
- A rundown of isolation patterns — schema recreating, transaction wrapping, table truncating, data namespacing, and Postgres template databases — with the trade-offs and a benchmark from a real project (800 tests, 28 tables, 184 columns)
